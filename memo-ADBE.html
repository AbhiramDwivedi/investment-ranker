## 2. Is the impairment real?

**Partly. The price umbrella is conceded; unit demand is not disproven by any disclosed number,
but Adobe's blended reporting also prevents it from being confirmed. Durn (CFO) has already left;
Narayen (CEO) has only announced he will leave once a successor is found - he has not left yet, and
overstating that timeline was this memo's first-draft error.**

*The concession is real; the exact size is this memo's estimate, not a filed number.* Adobe's
original FY26 target was 10.2% ending-ARR growth explicitly excluding Semrush (Dec. 10 8-K,
guidance table). The June 11 target is the identical 10.2%, now *including* the acquisition, with
ARR exiting Q2 at $27.10B including ~$480M from Semrush - that figure is Semrush's contribution
*at Q2 quarter-end*, not necessarily its year-end contribution, since Adobe also revalues ARR
annually for FX and can attribute cross-product entitlements to either customer group. Holding the
$480M flat to year end (an assumption, not a filed fact) against the $25.66B revalued FY25 base
implies ~8.3% organic growth versus the original 10.2% target - about 1.9 points lower. What is
filed and not estimated: the headline stayed at 10.2% while what counts toward it changed. Management
separately sized an organic ARR reduction at ~$500M on the Q2 call (secondary transcript - a
different figure from, and not reconcilable to, the filed $480M Semrush contribution) - deferred
Creative Cloud price increases and freemium spend.

*Name the mechanism - and read the blended line honestly.* Gross margin 89.4% vs 87.8% three years
ago shows delivery economics remain attractive, not that pricing power is intact; margin is usually
the last thing to move. S&M did move: 28.5% of revenue in Q2 FY26 vs 27.7% a year ago (filed,
$1.884B/$6.618B vs $1.626B/$5.873B), while R&D held near 18%. Adobe attributes the S&M increase
mainly to advertising and, secondarily, compensation (10-Q MD&A) - consistent with funnel defense,
but also with planned campaigns and Semrush integration spend, so an 80bp move is suggestive, not
proof. Creative & Marketing Professionals subscription revenue grew 13% (11% cc) to $4.54B - but
this is a blended customer-group line spanning Creative, Experience Platform, Experience Manager
and GenStudio, with cross-product entitlements attributable to either group, so 13% growth cannot
by itself refute seat loss or churn inside Creative Cloud specifically. Freemium MAU rose to ~90M
from ~50M (secondary transcript). Honest read: no disclosed metric proves core Creative unit loss,
but Adobe's blended reporting also prevents disproving it - itself a reason for wait, not pass.

*One segment, decomposed as far as disclosure allows.* Adobe consolidated Digital Media, Digital
Experience and Publishing & Advertising into one reportable segment in Q1 FY26 (10-Q Note 15); the
finest disclosed cut is the two customer-group revenue lines used above.

*Normalize GAAP EPS - and get the tax treatment right.* TTM GAAP EPS $17.47 carries three items
filed within the TTM window: a $70M/$0.17-per-share Q2 FY26 goodwill impairment (Publishing &
Advertising) and $62M/$0.15 (Q1) plus $30M/$0.07 (Q2) litigation settlements - no comparable items
in Q3 or Q4 FY25, the other two TTM quarters (same reconciliation table, filed). The 10-Q MD&A
states the impairment "is not deductible for income tax purposes," so its $0.17 addback needs no
tax haircut - pretax equals after-tax there. The litigation charges are not disclosed as
non-deductible; applying an assumed ~21% rate (Adobe's GAAP effective rate was 24% this quarter,
its fixed non-GAAP rate 18%) to the combined $0.22 gives an after-tax addback of ~$0.17. Normalized
TTM EPS: ~$17.80 - an estimate, not filed, since Adobe nets every adjustment's tax effect into one
combined line and discloses no per-item rate. That is below the $17.86 pretax figure this memo's
first draft used (which wrongly treated the whole $0.39 as needing no haircut); the multiple still
moves from the reported 15.80x to ~15.5x normalized - marginally cheaper, a smaller correction than
first claimed. Separately, the impairment itself is adverse evidence, not a neutral add-back: Adobe
tests goodwill on "reduced future cash-flow estimates and slower growth rates," and recorded this
one specifically against Publishing & Advertising.

*SBC-adjusted FCF, not a footnote.* Reported FCF $10.28B is ~9.4% of market cap; SBC-adjusted FCF
is $8.249B, 7.5% (Section 0, engine-computed). Share count fell 12.3% over three years against 8.3%
of market cap spent on buybacks at a dollar-weighted 41st percentile of own history - real net
retirement, not offsetting SBC issuance. ROIC 55.4% is computed on invested capital ($12.97B) that
includes goodwill and is depressed by roughly a decade of buybacks; directionally strong, not a
precise return on a stable capital base.

*One peer, current multiple - growth caveat stated, not omitted.* Autodesk (ADSK), governed close
$253.83 (build/universe.json), trades at 37.1x TTM P/E against Adobe's 15.80x reported / ~15.5x
normalized. Some of that ~2.4x gap is earned: Autodesk grew total net revenue 18% in FY2026 (ended
Jan. 31, 2026, its own 10-K), faster than Adobe's 11-13% cc customer-group growth or this memo's
~8.3% Adobe organic estimate, with net revenue retention above 100-110%. Autodesk's own GAAP
earnings carry a heavy SBC load - $788M against $1,124M of net income, ~70% - so 37.1x is itself
partly SBC-inflated, not a clean read either. Net of both effects: a >2x gap against a faster-
growing, similarly AI-exposed comparable is more discount than growth alone explains, but this is
one directional data point, not proof Adobe specifically is mispriced.

*What the price implies, and the replay.* `at_hurdle_20x_exit` +6.4% (engine, unnormalized GAAP TTM
base) against 8.65% trailing five-year CAGR delivered while the multiple collapsed - the band is
rejected on that gap, not on hope. The 0-for-3 replay (median 12m excess -32.5%) fired when the
*absolute* forward multiple was 30-40x, a bubble-era comp; today's 10.0x forward non-GAAP / ~15.5x
normalized GAAP, and the Autodesk gap above, suggest today's setup differs, though not conclusively
- hence kill criteria on ARR and margin, never on multiple alone.

## 4. Kill criteria - pre-committed, falsifiable, dated

1. **Total Adobe ending ARR versus Adobe's own FY26 target - a filed target-cut test plus a
   same-basis pace check, not a synthetic organic growth rate.** Sept. 10, 2026 (Q3 FY26). Adobe
   has never disclosed a "Total Adobe ARR" figure for Q3 FY25: the combined-segment ARR metric
   began only with the Dec. 10, 2025 8-K's disclosure change (Q3 FY25 reported "Digital Media ARR"
   $18.59B instead), so no same-point year-over-year comparison is possible from EDGAR filings.
   This memo's first-draft test compared a nine-month-elapsed ARR change against a full-year-rate
   threshold, which is not a like-for-like comparison and is dropped. Replaced with two checks on
   filed numbers only: (a) if Adobe cuts the FY26 ending-ARR growth target below 10.2% -
   re-underwrite (a direct same-metric, same-scope comparison against the Dec. 10, 2025 and June 11,
   2026 targets, no bridging). (b) if Adobe reaffirms 10.2% but Q3 sequential ARR growth (Q3 exiting
   ARR minus the $27.10B Q2 FY26 actual) is under half the pro-rata pace needed for the remaining
   two quarters (about $590M/quarter, implied by 10.2% of the $25.66B FY25 base, so under ~$295M
   added in Q3) - review, not re-underwrite. Benign exception: Adobe's ARR bookings may be
   back-half-weighted, a pattern common to enterprise software though not yet confirmed specifically
   for Adobe; running behind a straight-line pace at Q3 without an accompanying target cut is not on
   its own proof of a growth problem.
2. **Creative & Marketing Professionals subscription revenue growth, constant currency - a lagging,
   blended proxy, not direct Creative Cloud evidence.** Sept. 10, 2026. Below 9% cc (Q2 FY26: 11%
   cc; Q4 FY25: 10% cc) - review; below 7% cc - re-underwrite, and only if corroborated by any
   Creative-specific pricing, net-new-ARR, or engagement data Adobe discloses at that time, since
   this line blends Creative with Experience Platform/Manager/GenStudio and Semrush. This is a
   same-metric, same-scope quarterly cc-growth figure Adobe itself discloses each quarter, so the
   period comparison is like-for-like; the caveat above is about what the metric covers, not how it
   is computed. Benign exception: Semrush integration or cross-product entitlement shifts dilute the
   reported cc growth rate without a deceleration in the pre-existing Creative business - hard to
   distinguish given Adobe's blended reporting, which is itself why this criterion is a review
   trigger, not a standalone kill.
3. **Non-GAAP operating margin, tiered and read jointly with the ARR signal in criterion 1.**
   Checked at the Q3 FY26 print (Sept. 10, 2026) and the initial FY27 guide, which Adobe would
   typically give alongside Q4/FY26 results (~Dec. 2026), not a stand-alone later date. Stable
   44-45% margin alongside a target cut or a pace shortfall under criterion 1 is itself a
   deterioration signal (demand softening, margin not yet absorbing the defense) - review. 42-44%
   alongside no adverse signal under criterion 1 reads as AI/freemium investment - review. Below
   42%, or any margin decline paired with an adverse signal under criterion 1 - re-underwrite.
   Benign exception: a guided, time-boxed step-down tied to a named AI-infrastructure investment
   with a stated payback.
4. **S&M as a share of revenue - a review signal, not a standalone kill.** Each quarterly report
   through Q2 FY27 (June 2027), using filed dollars (Q2 FY26: 28.5% of revenue). Above ~29% for two
   consecutive quarters AND an adverse signal under criterion 1 (a target cut or pace shortfall) -
   review; three consecutive quarters under both conditions - re-underwrite. Elevated S&M alone,
   without a matching ARR signal, is not sufficient on its own. Benign exception: a one-quarter
   step-up tied to a specific product launch that coincides with on-pace ARR is investment, not
   defense.
5. **CEO succession and initial FY27 guidance, on filed economic thresholds, checked separately.**
   No permanent CEO named by the Q1 FY27 report (~March 2027) - review, governance discount.
   Separately, at the Q4/FY26 report (~Dec. 2026, when Adobe would typically give its first FY27
   guidance): initial FY27 targets implying Total Adobe ARR growth below 8% year over year off the
   FY26 actual base (a full-fiscal-year-to-full-fiscal-year comparison - like-for-like once Semrush
