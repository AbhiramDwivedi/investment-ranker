## 1. What changed — why is the multiple where it is?

On July 30, AMG reported Q2 diluted EPS of $6.95 and economic EPS of $8.29, up 54% year over year. AUM reached $942.4 billion and quarterly net client inflows were $12.9 billion. Private markets and liquid alternatives added $28.9 billion, while equities lost $14.5 billion and multi-asset and fixed income lost $1.5 billion. Aggregate fees rose 42% in Q2. The filing attributes $437.5 million, or 37 percentage points, of that increase to asset-based fees and $58.7 million, or five percentage points, to performance-based fees. First-half aggregate fees rose 46% to $3.57 billion; a derived annualized fee rate increased about 11.7 basis points, but that is memo arithmetic, not a company-reported metric. TTM GAAP earnings also include about $100 million after tax from Peppertree in Q3 2025, $183.3 million from Comvest and Montrusco in Q4 2025 and $11.0 million from myCIO in Q2 2026. These disposal gains total about $294 million, or 34% of calculated TTM controlling net income of $856.7 million.

## 2. Is the impairment real?

The impairment case is mixed. Alternatives produced $57.8 billion of first-half inflows, but about $69 billion of the $171 billion increase in AUM over 12 months came from new Affiliates. AMG spent $242.3 million on Affiliate investments, so growth reflects organic flows and purchased assets. Differentiated long-only equities had $14.5 billion of Q2 outflows.

AMG reports one segment, but its disclosed Affiliate groups have different economics. Consolidated Affiliate revenue was $1.19 billion in the first half, up 20%. Equity-method income was $272.2 million, up 93%, while related revenue rose 64%. Equity-method pre-tax earnings of $343.0 million were most of total pre-tax income of $500.2 million. About 39% of equity-method AUM could earn performance fees, versus 12% at consolidated Affiliates. The alternatives-heavy minority interests therefore drive more earnings variability than the consolidated group.

Reported TTM GAAP EPS is $28.57. Removing roughly 34% attributable to disposal gains gives a normalized GAAP base near $18.75. TTM economic EPS of $32.10 is a different basis: it excludes transaction gains, intangible amortization, related deferred taxes and Affiliate equity items. A $30.00 economic-EPS base applies a 7% haircut for performance-fee normalization. Management provides no quantitative EPS guidance, and consensus growth estimates could not be verified.

Reported TTM free cash flow is $1.065 billion, but first-half operating cash flow includes $464.5 million of distributions from equity-method investments against $272.2 million of booked equity-method income. That $192.3 million excess is cash, but its recurrence depends on Affiliate distributions. A broader reinvestment view also subtracts $242.3 million of first-half Affiliate investments. The engine withholds SBC-adjusted FCF because its TTM SBC sign is unverified. The filing identifies only $17.1 million of first-half share-based compensation, versus $66.7 million of Affiliate equity expense; they are economically distinct. AMG deployed $936.1 million across repurchases, convertible repayments and conversion payments, and $56.7 million of Affiliate equity purchases, while adding $650 million of net revolver debt. Net debt was about $2.59 billion, or 2.05x annualized first-half adjusted EBITDA attributable to controlling interests.

T. Rowe Price at about 10.9x GAAP TTM earnings is a secondary traditional-manager reference. It can anchor only the normalized-GAAP table, not an economic-EPS valuation. Current alternatives-peer multiples could not be verified. The engine's 7.8% required growth uses reported GAAP EPS and its own historical median; the economic and normalized-GAAP tables below are separate diagnostics.
