## 1. What changed - why is the multiple where it is?

On November 6, 2025, Brighthouse agreed to Aquarian Capital's approximately $4.1 billion acquisition for $70 cash per common share. Shareholders approved it on February 12, 2026. The Hart-Scott-Rodino waiting period expired, FINRA approved the transaction on May 19 and the Committee on Foreign Investment in the United States concluded its review on June 18. Delaware, New York and Massachusetts insurance approvals remained outstanding in the August 6 Form 10-Q.

The merger agreement's original September 6 outside date automatically extends to December 6 when only those insurance approvals remain open. Financing is not a closing condition. Aquarian has equity and debt commitment letters. Parent owes a $225.5 million reverse termination fee in specified Parent-fault outcomes, while Brighthouse may owe $143.5 million in specified company-fault outcomes. The agreement bars common dividends and repurchases before closing or termination.

At the September 2 close of $53.62, the stock was 23.4% below contractual consideration. Regulatory execution and the break price, rather than ordinary multiple mean reversion, now determine the return.

## 2. Is the impairment real?

The live question is regulatory, not simply whether the franchise is impaired. The proxy makes approval free of a Parent-defined Burdensome Condition a closing condition. Delaware, New York and Massachusetts can therefore approve the acquisition with capital-maintenance or other conditions that Aquarian refuses, even without a formal disapproval. The filings do not explain why those reviews remain open, so the memo cannot assign a narrow closing probability without the state Form A dockets and hearing calendars.

Standalone evidence is mixed. Shield Level Annuity sales reached $2.119 billion in Q2, up 10.1% year over year, but first-half Shield sales rose only 2.7% to $3.987 billion. Total Q2 annuity sales fell 7% to $2.425 billion, and fixed and income annuity sales fell to $133 million from $505 million. This can reflect spread-driven pricing discipline rather than lost demand.

Combined risk-based capital rose to 430%-450% from 405%-425% a year earlier, but total adjusted capital fell 12% from $5.560 billion to $4.900 billion over the five-quarter span. It was relatively flat sequentially in Q2, so this was not five consecutive declines. Holding-company liquid assets were $0.9 billion, unchanged from a year earlier. As an inference from disclosed capital and ratio data, required capital fell faster than total adjusted capital.

GAAP EPS of $12.53 and the engine's revenue growth are distorted by hedge and market-risk-benefit marks. Adjusted revenue fell 2.5% to $4.201 billion in the first half. Adjusted EPS less notable items totaled $17.27 over four quarters, but it systematically excludes recurring derivative effects; first-half net derivative losses were $986 million. A conservative normalized starting EPS of $14 reflects that limitation without treating all hedge marks as economic loss. Reported TTM free cash flow was negative $538 million, but consolidated GAAP free cash flow is not a useful life-insurer measure because policyholder flows are financing activities. The SBC expense needed for an adjusted figure was not verified, so SBC-adjusted FCF remains unverified.

For a signed cash deal, the useful comparators are the proxy's $51.09 undisturbed price and $43.36 52-week low, not noisy peer P/E figures. Goldman's 0.25x-0.45x range was a terminal price-to-book-ex-AOCI assumption applied to projected book value, not a current fairness range.

Use an event gate: all three approvals without a Burdensome Condition and a firm closing date, or resolution on December 6. The machine's $29.44 low rung includes preferred equity and is not an entry.
