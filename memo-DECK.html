## 2. Is the impairment real?

**The earnings base needs no normalization.** No merger fee, impairment, or
disposal gain in FY2026 or Q1 FY2027 GAAP results; the Koolaburra/AHNU
wind-down carried "no material exit costs" (10-K). TTM EPS $7.07 and FY2026
$7.02 are used as reported.

**SBC-adjusted FCF is a real haircut but a small one, and the cash-flow
timing is ordinary.** Reported FCF (TTM) $1,117.8M; stock-based compensation
(SBC)-adjusted $1,071.0M (`sbc_to_fcf` 4.0%), yielding 8.6% of market cap
against roughly 9.0% on the reported figure (both from section 0). Q1
FY2027's operating cash flow was thin ($47.9M) because a $341.6M
accounts-payable inflow largely offset a $320.6M inventory build (10-Q); the
same pattern (smaller scale) shows in the prior-year quarter, so this reads
as Deckers' ordinary seasonal financing of UGG's pre-holiday inventory
build, not a one-time source propping up the TTM FCF figure the SBC
adjustment above is applied to.

**Marketing spend and price promotion are different mechanisms - keep them
separate.** Total advertising/marketing/promotion expense rose from 8.48% of
revenue in Q1 FY2026 to 9.03% in Q1 FY2027 ($81.8M/$964.5M to
$92.1M/$1,019.5M, 10-Q) - a real, modest rise in customer-acquisition
intensity, the metric the template's own framework flags for a subscription
or platform business, here applied to a branded-goods one. That is a cost-
of-growth signal, not itself evidence of discounting. Price promotion is a
separate question: the FY2026 10-K says promotional activity "slightly
increased" from "exceptionally low levels" in the prior year, but the
company still achieved "high levels of full-price sell through" and
"largely maintained" gross margin, and flags it "may not realize similar
gross margin benefits" in FY2027. That is a genuine forward risk, not yet a
filed impairment - Q1 FY2027 gross margin actually rose 60bp, and DTC
comparable sales (+6.8%, which strips new-store effects and is the closest
disclosed proxy to full-price demand) read as healthy, not eroding. The
quarter's unit and price data corroborate this rather than undercut it:
consolidated units fell 1.4% (10-Q) while net sales rose 5.7% and gross
margin rose 60bp - a units-down/price-up/margin-up combination consistent
with premiumization and full-price selling, not with discount-driven volume
chasing. Both signals are real and both are shown; neither yet shows the
loss of pricing power the FY2026 warning flags as a risk for FY2027.

**Segment decomposition.** Q1 FY2027 segment operating margin: HOKA 36.3%
(was 38.8%, -250bp), UGG 19.4% (was 20.4%, -100bp), Other 17.2% (was 16.7%,
now immaterial post-wind-down); total segment margin 31.0% (was 32.7%,
-170bp). Unallocated corporate costs rose faster than segment income -
$149.977M to $160.723M, +7.2% - adding roughly another 20bp of
consolidated-margin drag on top. HOKA is still highly profitable even after
the compression; the corporate-overhead line grew about as fast as the
brand-level pressure did.

**Concentration is real and decides the tier.** HOKA plus UGG were 97.3% of
FY2026 net sales ($2,587.3M + $2,738.8M of $5,472.3M). Channel and
geographic diversification does not offset a loss of consumer relevance at
either brand, since both would be hit together in that scenario. Section 3
uses `cyclical_or_single_product`/12% rather than the engine's default
`quality_compounder`/9% on this basis alone.

**Buybacks: use the net share count, not the gross yield.** The
candidate_reasons text's "9.8% buyback yield ... enough to fund about +10% a
year" divides dollars spent by market cap; it does not net the price paid,
offsetting employee issuance, or timing. The engine's own
`share_count_chg_3y` is -12.3% over three years, about -4.3%/yr compounded -
a real net reduction, materially below the raw yield figure, and the number
section 3's bridge uses.

**The US slowdown: cyclical or structural is not yet distinguishable, and
the disclosed evidence is mixed, not silent.** Domestic net sales growth
(+0.2% FY2026, +3.2% Q1 FY2027) is the clearest filed warning. Corrected
from an earlier draft, which wrongly said Deckers does not disclose unit
volume: the 10-Q reports consolidated units sold down 1.4% to 14,500
thousand from 14,700 thousand, and HOKA brand net sales by channel - DTC
+17.3% to $256.8M, wholesale +2.9% to $446.8M (10-Q). Deckers still does not
disclose brand-level domestic growth, US-specific wholesale or DTC trends,
or DTC traffic - the geography-by-brand cut that would most directly settle
whether the US slowdown is cyclical or structural. What it does disclose,
enterprise-wide, leans toward intact demand: HOKA's DTC channel, the one
closest to end consumers, grew far faster than its wholesale channel, and

sell-in. The 1.4% unit decline against 5.7% revenue growth implies average
selling price rose roughly 7%, and the prior-year unit count itself includes
volume from the since-phased-out Koolaburra and AHNU brands, so the
like-for-like decline is smaller than the headline. None of this is
US-specific, so it does not resolve the domestic-growth warning - it is
enterprise-wide evidence that reads as premiumization and channel mix, not
as the promotional, volume-chasing pattern a real US HOKA maturation would
show. International growth (+26.8% FY2026, +8.4% Q1 FY2027) and consolidated
