## 2. Is the impairment real?

**Normalize the base first (review finding 1).** Q1 FY27 GAAP EPS $5.24
included a $631M / $0.96-per-share fair-value gain on a strategic equity
investment (Dell's reconciliation, 8-K Ex-99.1). The FY27 guidance bridge
shows the same mechanism annualized: GAAP EPS $17.31 midpoint to non-GAAP
$17.90, with a -$0.97/share "fair value adjustments on equity investments"
line; a footnote says no further gain or loss is being forecast for the rest
of the year, so that -$0.97 is essentially the Q1 gain carried into the
full-year guide. TTM GAAP EPS $12.56 (Section 0) carries the same item across
four quarters: FY26 full year -$0.37/share, Q1 FY26 -$0.02/share, Q1 FY27
-$0.96/share, netting to about $1.31/share, ~10% of the reported base.
Normalized: TTM EPS ~$11.25 (12.56 - 1.31); FY27 guided GAAP EPS ~$16.34
(17.31 - 0.97). No tax bridge is applied to either subtraction - Dell's own
reconciliation nets the tax effect of every adjustment line (amortization,
SBC, other corporate items, the investment gain) into one combined row, so
the investment-gain line cannot be isolated after tax without an assumed
rate; using the full pre-tax figure is conservative if anything (some of that
amount would have been taxed away before removal, which would raise the
normalized EPS, not lower it). Non-GAAP EPS ($4.86 Q1; $17.90 FY27 guide) is
not the fix - it also strips SBC, amortization and other corporate costs.
The consequence cuts against the bull case: DELL's forward multiple on the
normalized $16.34 base is ~27.1x (442.08/16.34), richer than the ~25.5x the
unnormalized $17.31 headline implies, because removing a gain lowers the
denominator. Normalized trailing P/E is ~39.3x (442.08/11.25) against the
engine's reported pe_ttm 35.20.

**Backlog is a filed direction, not a filed level (finding 2).** The
Q4/FY26 8-K states entering-FY27 backlog of $43B; the Q1 FY27 10-Q (filed
June 9) states only "a significant increase in backlog as we exited the
[Q1] quarter" - no dollar figure anywhere. Booking $24.4B against $16.1B
recognized in Q1 is filed and consistent with a net build, but any specific
ending balance (this memo's first draft used ~$51B) is this memo's own
bookings-minus-revenue arithmetic, one-for-one, assuming no cancellations, no
scope changes, and no direct-from-order shipments - an estimate, not a
disclosure. Section 4 no longer keys a kill criterion to that undisclosed
level.

**Cash conversion is working-capital-driven, not proof either way (finding
3).** FY26 operating cash flow ($11.185B, 10-K) leaned on a $12.665B increase
in accounts payable against $7.022B of receivables growth and $3.987B of
inventory growth; Q1 FY27 ($4.081B OCF) repeated the pattern - $11.661B of
A/P growth against $8.331B of receivables and $4.715B of inventory growth
(both filed, 10-Q). That does not prove FCF is inflated (the offsets are real
cash), but it does make near-term FCF a payment-timing number more than an
earnings-power proxy - the engine's own multi-year view shows it:
cash_conversion 1.12 (fcf/ni TTM) but cash_conversion_3y_avg 2.15, a wide
gap that is the working-capital swing showing up in a ratio. Engine TTM FCF
$9.442B, SBC-adjusted $8.720B (3.0% of market cap, sbc_to_fcf 8.0%) - the

**Name the mechanism, decompose the segments (finding 4).** The threat named
in Section 1 is thin, concentrated AI-server gross margins; the metric that
would show it first is ISG operating margin and AI-server gross-profit
dollars, not consolidated gross-margin percent, which falls mechanically as
a bigger, lower-margin AI mix dilutes a rising blend even while operating

year ago); CSG operating margin 8.0% (vs 5.2%); traditional servers and
networking +92%, storage +8%, CSG +17%. That is expansion, not erosion, in
the freshest filed quarter - FY26's CSG operating income -5% YoY is a real
prior-year data point, but the newest quarter moved the other way. Per the
Q1 FY27 earnings-call transcript (secondary; not an EDGAR filing), management
guided Q2 CSG operating margin toward roughly 6%, a sequential step down
worth checking against the filed number (Section 4).

## 4. Kill criteria - pre-committed, falsifiable, dated

Kill criteria are dated to Dell's confirmed Q2 FY27 event, **Sept. 1, 2026**
(secondary-sourced date, confirmed via Dell's Aug. 18, 2026 conference-call
announcement), not the "last week of August" this memo's first draft
assumed before the date was confirmed.

1. **AI-optimized server bookings vs. recognized AI-server revenue, paired
   with management's own backlog language.** Review after one quarter where
   disclosed AI bookings fall below AI-server revenue and management
   describes backlog as lower than the prior quarter; re-underwrite after
   two consecutive such quarters or a cut to the AI-server revenue outlook.
   Benign exception: bookings can fall below revenue in a quarter where
   backlog converts on schedule and shipments outpace bookings without
   demand weakening.
2. **Non-GAAP gross margin, paired with ISG operating margin.** Review if
   non-GAAP gross margin falls more than 150bp sequentially AND ISG
   operating margin falls sequentially in the same quarter; re-underwrite if
   that pairing also comes with a cut to FY27 revenue or EPS guidance.
   Benign exception: gross-margin percent can fall mechanically as a bigger,
   lower-margin AI-server mix dilutes a rising blend even while gross-profit
   dollars and operating income keep growing.
3. **FY27 revenue or AI-server-revenue guidance.** Review if either range is
   reduced (not merely held flat) from the May 28, 2026 raise ($167B / ~$60B
   midpoints); re-underwrite if the cut is paired with weaker bookings or a
   lower reported backlog. Benign exception: a shipment- or component-
   timing-driven trim with orders and backlog intact is a materially
   different signal from a demand-driven cut.
4. **CSG (Client Solutions Group) quarterly operating margin, tiered.**
   Review below 5.5%; re-underwrite below 5.0%; treat two consecutive
   quarters at roughly 4.5%-5.0% alongside weak commercial-client revenue
   growth as evidence of structural erosion. Checked each quarterly report
   starting Q2 FY27 (Sept. 1, 2026) through the FY27 10-K (~March 2027).
   Benign exception: a single quarter's dip tied to a promotion or mix shift
   ahead of a product refresh is not the same as sustained erosion.
