## 4. Kill criteria — dated and falsifiable

1. **U.S. revenue growth, tiered, with a quality check.** Review after any
   quarter below +9% YoY; re-underwrite after two consecutive quarters below
   +9%. Also review if growth stays above 9% but management attributes it
   mainly to price/utilization rather than the net-customer-addition volume
   this memo can currently trace only to a full-year-2025 figure (600,000-
   700,000 net adds, excluding Stelo). Checked each quarterly earnings 8-K
   and 10-Q MD&A, starting Oct. 29, 2026.
2. **CMS non-insulin Type 2 coverage decision, tiered on the CMS
   determination itself, not a management characterization of it.** Denial
   or materially narrowed coverage (specific population, effective date,
   utilization conditions and reimbursement terms all disclosed by CMS)
   impairs the thesis; a delay past Dec. 31, 2026 with no adverse CMS signal
   updates the timing and probability of the incremental covered lives in
   the long-range plan rather than triggering an automatic haircut; broad
   coverage at reimbursement below commercial rates needs a fresh economics
   read. A Dexcom management statement on the Q4 2026 call is supporting
   color, not a substitute for the CMS decision. Checked via CMS's
   coverage-determination database, with the Q4 2026 call (~Feb. 2027) as
   corroboration only.
3. **Competitive-bidding single payment amount, two-stage.** Stage one:
   record the winning single payment amount and contract status once CMS
   announces it — the FY2025 10-K states only that contracting begins in
   2027 with payment changes effective January 1, 2028, and specifies no
   announcement date. Stage two: because DexCom does not disclose
   Medicare-channel revenue, a >20%-cut trigger cannot be weighted against
   actual exposure until management provides a bridge to revenue and
   operating income; request or wait for that bridge before re-underwriting
   on the headline percentage alone. A smaller headline cut concentrated in
   supplier eligibility or contract terms could still matter.
4. **Non-GAAP gross margin, tiered as a manufacturing-economics test, not
   evidence the moat is intact.** Below 62.0% for two consecutive quarters,
   AND the shortfall not fully explained by the previously guided Q3 peak /
   Ireland-ramp step-down. This test catches manufacturing inefficiency or
   quality-remediation cost; competitive damage from price, rebates or mix
   is more likely to show up first in revenue per customer or growth (kill
   criterion 1) than in consolidated gross margin. Checked each earnings 8-K
   starting Oct. 29, 2026.
5. **Product-quality escalation to a material outcome, not a pleading-stage
   event.** A second FDA warning letter or a Class I recall of a DexCom
   sensor; or a G8 FDA submission/approval delay specifically tied to
   unresolved quality-system deficiencies from the March 2025 letter; or,
   in the SDNY securities case or the consolidated G6/G7 consumer
   litigation, a material reserve, settlement, adjudicated finding, discovery
   of new product-quality facts, or an operational restriction. A denied
   motion to dismiss alone does not trigger this criterion — that is a
   normal litigation-stage event, not evidence on the merits. Checked via
   FDA's warning-letter/recall databases and DexCom's quarterly 10-Q
   litigation disclosures, through 2027.
