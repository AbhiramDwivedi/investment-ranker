## 2. Is the impairment real?

**Partly. The disclosed weakness is real and presently concentrated in the DIY tax funnel - an
undisclosed subset of TurboTax's ~30% of company revenue, not the whole 30% - while no filed
evidence yet shows material deterioration in Global Business Solutions or the rest of Consumer.**
Management does not dispute the concession - it will "evolve our DIY business model to
deliver the right value at the right price point" at the low end (May 20 call) - and the named
AI-native entrants are real: Prime Meridian (free federal/state filing, former IRS commissioner
Danny Werfel advising) and Perplexity's $17/month tax agent both launched into the 2026 season and
did not exist a year ago.

*Decomposed by segment and product line, not by TAM.* This memo's first draft argued DIY was "only"
12% of a self-reported $5 billion TAM inside a $42 billion category - the review is right that TAM
share is not revenue exposure, and that framing understated what is actually at risk. The filed
numbers: nine months ended April 30, 2026, Consumer segment revenue $7.654 billion (+10%) -
TurboTax $5.143 billion (+7%, about 30% of total company revenue on its own), Credit Karma $1.898
billion (+22%), ProTax $613 million (+4%); Global Business Solutions $9.440 billion (+17%) - Online
Ecosystem $7.315 billion (+20%, QuickBooks Online Accounting $3.732 billion +24%, Online
Services/money and payroll $3.583 billion +17%) and Desktop Ecosystem $2.125 billion (+7%). Both
segments report near-identical 75-77% segment operating margins, but that figure is not fully loaded:
Intuit excludes about $7.6 billion of nine-month unallocated corporate items (SBC, acquired-intangible
amortization, customer success and product development managed at the platform level - 10-Q MD&A)
from segment operating income, so the 75-77% pair cannot by itself show the two segments have
equivalent underlying economics - it shows only that neither segment is visibly worse before that
corporate overlay. TurboTax alone at ~30% of revenue is material, not a rounding error - the DIY
subset of it is where the concession sits, which is part of why the verdict below is wait, not an
unconditional pass. QuickBooks
Online Accounting - arguably the GBS product most exposed to agentic bookkeeping in principle - is
Intuit's fastest-growing major line at +24% nine-month growth: not evidence AI competitive pressure
is absent from GBS (automation can attack tax and accounting at once, as the review notes, and
Intuit does not disclose enough at the segment level to rule that out), but no disclosed number
shows it happening yet either. Credit Karma is a lender-marketing business, not a subscription
relationship - its growth is driven by personal-loan, credit-card and insurance vertical demand
(filed) and is credit-cycle sensitive, not a durable moat by itself. Mailchimp is not broken out
from Online Ecosystem in any filing this memo found; its standalone growth, retention and
AI-competitive exposure could not be verified.

*Name the mechanism - and reject the false-precision bear case while keeping the real one.*
Goldman's $0.12-per-return AI inference cost against roughly $162 of TurboTax revenue per return
(secondary source) ignores customer acquisition, identity verification and fraud prevention, tax-law
maintenance and testing, audit support, e-filing infrastructure, regulatory compliance and liability
allocation - a complete, audit-supported return does not cost twelve cents to deliver, and that
specific number is rejected while the broader substitution risk it gestures at is kept. The
better-evidenced version: TurboTax Online paying units are guided +2% for FY26 against ARPU +11%
(both disclosed in the May 20 earnings release - furnished with the 8-K, not "filed" for Exchange Act purposes) - unit growth funded almost entirely by price and mix, not volume - while total
TurboTax Online units are guided -2% and pay-nothing customers fall to roughly 7 million from 8
million: real paying-unit growth out of a shrinking, more-monetized funnel, consistent with either
"winning the customers worth keeping" or "losing the free-tier funnel that used to convert."
Gross margin does not resolve it either way. The engine's own
`gross_margin` field is unavailable for INTU - the underlying EDGAR tag is stale since July 2020
(Section 0 data notes) - so this memo computed it directly from the filed cost-of-revenue lines: Q3
FY26 gross margin was 83.9% against 84.6% a year ago (down about 0.7 point), while the nine-month
figure was 80.7% against 80.3% (up about 0.4 point) - under a point either way, not a signal, which
is why gross margin is the wrong place to look for a subscription/platform business. Company-wide
S&M was flat to slightly down as a share of revenue (nine-month 24.98% against 25.23% a year ago; Q3
alone 20.95% against 20.87%, both filed) - no visible ramp in defensive marketing spend yet, though
Intuit does not break out segment-level opex, so a TurboTax-specific ramp offset by GBS discipline
would not show up here. R&D rose modestly as a share of revenue in both windows (nine-month 14.74%
against 14.18%; Q3 9.82% against 9.12%), consistent with, not proof of, AI reinvestment. TurboTax Live growing 36% to $2.8 billion and to 53% of TurboTax
revenue (disclosed guide, same earnings release) is real evidence of a shift toward assisted offerings, but assisted tax is not automatically insulated from AI: the same technology that makes
Intuit's own experts faster can also let a competitor offer human-reviewed returns more cheaply, or
compress Intuit's own take rate even as Live revenue keeps growing. No disclosed metric - expert
cost per return, contribution margin per Live customer, ARPU trend within Live specifically -
answers that question either way; it is a genuine open question, not a resolved bull point, and
kill criterion 4 below is built to catch it.

*The restructuring is an execution risk, not just an EPS input.* A 17% headcount cut is disruptive
on its own terms - product velocity, support quality, expert-service capacity (TurboTax Expert
Assist/Full Service and QuickBooks Live both depend on staffed experts) and retention are plausible
casualties, and Intuit has not disclosed a bridge from gross savings to net EPS benefit. CFO Sandeep
Aujla's own framing - "disciplined capital allocation... investing in our Big Bets while
consistently returning capital" - signals savings will be partly reinvested, not fully dropped to
EPS. This memo does not assume the full $300-340 million flows through; the Aug. 25 print and FY27
guide are the first checkable evidence (Section 4).

*SBC-adjusted FCF, not a footnote.* Reported TTM FCF is $7.725 billion, a ~7.7% yield on today's
market cap; SBC-adjusted FCF (engine-computed, Section 0) is $5.686 billion, a 5.7% yield -

cash generated for shareholders. Full-year FY26 SBC is guided at approximately $2.1 billion (Table E
footnote [c], disclosed - earnings release); nine-month actual SBC was $1.549 billion (10-Q footnote,
filed). Share count fell only 2.5% over three years despite the buyback program (`share_count_chg_3y`,

employee-plan issuance in this window, with shares outstanding falling 279.129 million to 274.268

4. Kill criteria

  1. Fiscal 2027 total-company revenue growth, initial guidance, read jointly with segment guidance (tiered). Checked at the Q4/FY26 report and initial FY27 guide, Aug. 25, 2026. Total growth at or above 11% is an all-clear only if BOTH segments individually guide growth at or above 9%; total growth at or above 11% carried mostly by one segment (the other below 9%) is review, not all-clear, since Credit Karma/payments strength could otherwise mask a weak tax or QuickBooks print. Total growth 9-11%: review. Below 9%: thesis impairment, downgrade to wait/re-underwrite, unless the shortfall is explained by a disclosed divestiture or reporting change (benign exception).
  2. Fiscal 2027 non-GAAP EPS growth, initial guidance, corroborated over time (not stand-alone). Guidance components checked at the Aug. 25, 2026 print; the SBC-adjusted FCF and share-count legs of the corroboration are not guided figures and are checked as subsequent quarterly filings post them. Guided non-GAAP EPS growth below 10% (FY26 guide: ~18%) trips the criterion outright at the Aug. 25 print. Guided at or above 10% is provisional until confirmed over the following two quarters by at least two of: total revenue growth at or above 9%; GAAP operating income growth roughly tracking revenue growth; SBC-adjusted FCF (this memo's engine-computed field, Section 0) growing year over year as of the next two quarterly filings; net diluted share count flat or declining. Response: downgrade to wait if the Aug. 25 guide itself fails; re-underwrite if the follow-up corroboration fails over the next two quarters. Benign exception: a guided step-down in non-GAAP EPS growth driven by a disclosed one-time reinvestment (a named AI or infrastructure program with a stated payback) alongside stable-or-better organic revenue growth.
  3. Online Ecosystem revenue growth, year over year (GBS sub-line: QuickBooks Online Accounting + Online Services), decomposed where possible. Checked at the next quarterly report, Q1 FY27, on or around Dec. 2026. Below 15% YoY (nine-month FY26: 20%; Q3 FY26: 19%) - review, since a drop from 20% toward 12% is already substantial damage and should not wait for a lower line to trip. Below 12%, or two consecutive quarters below 15% - re-underwrite. Where Intuit discloses it, corroborate against QuickBooks Online Accounting growth specifically (pricing/mix could otherwise mask a customer-count slowdown the same way it currently does in TurboTax). Benign exception: a single quarter's deceleration driven entirely by payments/payroll volume timing (money and payroll offerings within Online Services), with QuickBooks Online Accounting core subscription revenue still growing at or above the trailing four-quarter average.
  4. TurboTax Live revenue growth, corroborated against customer growth AND the spread between them. Checked at the Q3 FY27 report (the tax-season quarter), around May 2027. FY26 guidance already implies Live revenue per customer declining slightly (36% revenue growth against 38% customer growth), so a revenue/customer-growth test alone is a franchise-growth test, not a profitability test. Revenue growth below 15% (management's own 15-20% floor) AND customer growth below 15% (FY26 guide: 38%) together trip re-underwrite; either one alone triggers review. Separately: the spread between customer growth and revenue growth widening to more than 5 points (customers growing materially faster than revenue, beyond FY26's ~2-point gap) - review, corroborated against any disclosed TurboTax ARPU, paying-unit or service-cost data, since a widening spread is the first place Live's own unit economics would show pressure even while both growth rates stay above 15%. Benign exception: revenue growth decelerates while customer growth holds at or above 30% and the spread does not widen materially, consistent with mix-shift toward lower-priced tiers rather than a loss of the assisted-tax moat.
  5. A cut to Consumer or Global Business Solutions segment revenue growth guidance, relative to the initial FY27 guide. This replaces a forward-EPS-revision system signal with the named economic facts a revision trend is meant to proxy. Checked at each quarterly report through Q3 FY27 (~May 2027), against the segment-level growth figures set in the initial FY27 guide (Aug. 25, 2026). Either segment's full-year growth guidance cut by more than 2 percentage points from the initial FY27 figure for that segment - re-underwrite. Benign exception: a cut explained by a disclosed divestiture, segment reclassification, or a quantified currency effect rather than a demand change.
