## 1. What changed — why is the multiple where it is?

Micron completed its $1.8 billion Powerchip Tongluo fab acquisition in March 2026 and expects shipments from mid-2027, expanding capacity. On June 25, the Q3 10-Q reported $41.456 billion of revenue as DRAM average selling prices rose in the low-60% range sequentially. Note 17 reported Q3 revenue of $13.769 billion for CMBU, $11.524 billion for CDBU, $11.521 billion for MCBU and $4.634 billion for AEBU. This was a cyclical increase in earnings power. Management guided fiscal 2026 capital spending, net of incentives, to about $27 billion and disclosed about $5 billion of remaining performance obligations. Those agreements extended visibility but capped upside. Netlist won a $445 million jury verdict that Micron is appealing, a discrete risk equal to 0.94% of $47.268 billion of nine-month net income. The Patent Trial and Appeal Board found the asserted claims unpatentable, and affirmance would preclude enforcement, though a separate HBM claim remains. An Aug. 26 filing reported three management changes. The next event is the fiscal 2026 release expected in late September, followed by the 10-K; Q4 contains 14 weeks.

## 2. Is the impairment real?

The impairment is the risk that peak pricing and margins are permanent. All four segments were pricing-led. Q3 CMBU revenue rose 78% sequentially with a 78% operating margin. CDBU rose 103% with an 83% margin, MCBU rose 49% with an 86% margin and AEBU rose 71% with a 75% margin. CMBU was the largest segment at 33% of revenue, but MCBU earned the highest margin despite falling bit shipments. Broad pricing power, rather than HBM mix alone, remains cyclical evidence.

The contracts improve the cycle but cap the bull case. The largest agreements generally cap existing-product prices near second-calendar-quarter 2026 market prices and carry a floor. Management expects price-band contracts to earn margins well above any prior-cycle quarterly peak even at floor prices. That could establish a higher trough, but the ceiling surrenders further pricing upside. The approximately $5 billion remaining performance obligation is a minimum-volume, minimum-price floor. It excludes agreements without fixed prices or bands and those of one year or less. It equals about 12% of Q3 revenue, with about one-third due within 12 months, so it does not quantify total durability.

Cash flow is stronger than reported. TTM FCF was $26.172 billion and SBC-adjusted FCF was $24.994 billion, a 2.3% yield. Nine-month FCF was $26.100 billion, but operating cash flow absorbed a $19.953 billion receivables increase and benefited from $10.671 billion of specified liability inflows. Normalizing both gives about $35.4 billion; the review's broader liability classification gives about $38.7 billion. Receivables of $31.025 billion imply about 68 days of sales, not yet a collection failure, while the $3.32 billion customer-consideration accrual is a quality risk.

Western Digital is an imperfect memory-adjacent peer. The Sept. 2 engine puts WDC at 18.5x trailing earnings versus MU at 21.6x, but WDC's $24.28 EPS includes a $6.498 billion Sandisk gain. Adjusting that gain and $907 million of related costs yields roughly $10.50 EPS and 42x. MU is not clearly expensive relative to WDC, but deserves a discount because its $44.18 EPS is also cyclical. MU's book multiple exceeds its engine band, but book is peak-lagged: equity rose from $54.165 billion to $100.724 billion in nine months. A normalized $18 EPS is generous at 3.16 times the $5.69 average of 14 filed TTM observations. Repurchases were negligible: $650 million was less than $762 million withheld for awards, shares rose from 1.122 billion to 1.129 billion and nine-month dividends were $437 million.

**Verdict: wait.** The $956.08 price exceeds a generous structural bull case built on normalized $18 EPS, and the conclusion survives using full peak earnings.

Entry: use the fiscal 2026 release expected in late September as the event gate, not the $198.63 low rung, because the band uses peak earnings and cash flow. The applicable tier is `cyclical_or_single_product` with a 12% hurdle; the engine's 10.6% figure uses a 9% hurdle.

- DRAM pricing: watch when MD&A reports one sequential DRAM ASP decline, re-underwrite after two consecutive declines if CMBU gross profit dollars also fall and consider pass after three such quarters. This uses the disclosed sequential ASP change, not an undisclosed contract-band midpoint.
- Contract floor economics: watch below a 55% quarterly GAAP gross margin, re-underwrite at or below the prior-cycle peak in the mid-40s and consider pass below 40%.
