## 2. Is the impairment real?

**Both. The de-rating is deserved as a re-rating, not as an impairment, and the cheapness reading in section 0 is now, by the engine's own admission, thin and single-basis.**

*Normalize before using the number, and get the tax treatment right -- three items, not one.* TTM GAAP EPS $3.18 = $0.59 (Q3'25) + $0.56 (Q4'25) + $1.23 (Q1'26) + $0.80 (Q2'26), on the Q1'26 10-Q's own filed diluted weighted-average share count of 4,298,437 thousand (this draft's prior estimate, backed out of net income/EPS, was 4,295 thousand -- close, but the filed figure replaces it below). Three one-time items sit inside that TTM window, two of them working the same direction as each other and against the fee. (i) Q1'26's $1.23 carries the $2.8B WBD termination fee, booked in "Interest and other income (expense)" (10-Q for the quarter ended March 31, 2026, Note 6 Acquisitions). Netflix's own MD&A attributes that quarter's effective-tax-rate jump (10% to 19%) partly to "a lower foreign-derived income deduction relative to the growth in income before income taxes" and partly to lower SBC tax benefits -- a plausible inference that the fee itself was taxed above the core business's rate, not a filed per-item rate; none is disclosed. (ii) Q3'25's 10-Q (filed Oct. 22, 2025) books a $619M "cumulative loss... related to non-income tax assessments with the Brazilian tax authorities" as an operating expense (cost of revenues) -- not flagged non-deductible anywhere in the filing, so it is treated as ordinarily deductible here and added back. (iii) The Q1'26 10-Q's interest-expense MD&A separately discloses "approximately $85 million... amortization of debt issuance costs... recognized in connection with the termination of financing arrangements associated with the WBD transaction" -- a real, filed, one-time interest charge from unwinding the aborted deal's financing, also added back. Using a consistent 19-25% assumed tax treatment across all three (no per-item rate is filed for any of them): after-tax fee $2.09-2.25B (subtract); after-tax Brazil addback $464-501M; after-tax financing-termination addback $64-69M. Net: normalized TTM EPS $2.79-2.82, call it $2.80 -- below the reported $3.18 but above this draft's earlier $2.67, because the two addbacks partly offset the fee. Normalized TTM P/E: ~28.4x against the reported pe_ttm 25.0x -- still pricier, not cheaper, once the fee is out, even after the more complete normalization.

*FCF, the same way, plus SBC -- and add back what the fee also depressed, not only what it inflated.* Reported TTM FCF is $11.152B; SBC-adjusted (section 0, engine-computed) is $10.665B, about 3.2% of market cap -- a small drag, TTM SBC is only about 4% of FCF. Separately, and larger: Q1'26 FCF of $5.094B holds the $2.8B pretax cash fee (10-Q MD&A: operating cash flow "increased...primarily driven by...the Warner Bros. termination fee"), but the SAME quarter's working-capital line also carries a $729M non-routine CASH payment of prior-period Brazil back-taxes (10-Q MD&A) that this draft's first pass left in, understating the normalized figure. Netting both: $11.152B - $2.8B fee + $0.729B Brazil cash payment = ~$9.08B before SBC; minus the ~$487M implied TTM SBC (the gap between reported and SBC-adjusted FCF above) gives ~$8.59B, roughly a 2.6% yield on the ~$333B market cap the engine's own SBC yield implies, against 3.3% reported and 3.2% SBC-only. Even $8.59B likely understates the true run rate: the Q2 letter separately notes Q2 FCF "included higher cash tax payments due in part to the Warner Bros. termination fee," an amount the filings do not size, so some of the fee's cash-tax drag sits inside Q2's reported FCF without an offsetting addback here -- $8.59B is a conservative floor, not a precise point estimate. Share count fell 5.6% over three years against about 8% of market cap spent on buybacks (section 0) -- real net retirement, not simply offsetting SBC issuance, though some of this year's buyback capacity was fee-funded rather than organic.

*What is structural, named by mechanism, not gross margin -- and by region, since the deceleration is not even.* Gross margin 49.1% vs 38.8% three years ago shows delivery economics, not pricing power -- it is usually the last thing to move. The mechanism that actually moved is regional constant-currency revenue growth, filed each quarter (Q2 letter regional table, Q2'25 through Q2'26): UCAN 15/17/18/14/10%, EMEA cc 16/15/15/12/11%, LATAM cc 23/20/20/18/16%, APAC cc roughly flat near 19/19/18%. Deceleration is broad -- every region cooled, this is not one region's problem -- but it is fastest and furthest along exactly in UCAN, the highest-ARPU, most mature region, and slowest in APAC, the least mature. Management's own account: "Q2 revenue growth of 10% reflects only a partial quarter impact from our recent price change, which has gone well and as expected" (Q2 letter) -- a specific, primary claim that pricing is driving the UCAN slowdown rather than demand failing, but it is management's own characterization; churn and downgrade-to-ad-tier data that would independently confirm or refute it are not disclosed (could not verify). Separately, total view hours rose only +1.5% in 2025 and +2% in H1'26, and Netflix is retiring that report to an annual cadence from 2027 -- a disclosure cut layered on the slowdown, not just the slowdown itself; Nielsen has YouTube at a record 13.8% of US TV time in May 2026 versus 10.8% a year earlier, Netflix 8.0% -- suggestive of Netflix losing ground to short-form video broadly, but there is no comparable prior-year Netflix TV-time figure in these sources, so this is corroborating context, not itself proof of Netflix's own decline. S&M rose to 7% of revenue from 6%, but the 10-Q attributes the increase to advertising-sales headcount -- building the monetization engine, not defending the core.

*Leverage is genuinely low; content commitments are a different, non-debt-like number worth reading on its own terms.* Net debt/EBITDA 0.51x and contractual debt of $17.6B principal and interest (10-Q contractual-obligations table) are the real leverage read, and both are conservative. Content obligations of $25.1B (up from $24.0B six months earlier, $11.9B due within 12 months, plus $19.6B not yet on the balance sheet at all) are a much larger number, but they are mostly ordinary replacement spend for a content library that generates the revenue line above -- summing them with debt into one $45.4B figure, as this draft's first pass did, overstates leverage by treating an operating input as a financing claim. The more decision-useful read of that same content line is the ratio the Q2 letter itself guides to: cash content spend running ~1.1x amortization for 2026. That ratio, watched alongside normalized FCF and the regional growth data above, would show content-efficiency deterioration (spending run-rate rising while revenue growth and normalized cash generation both slow) well before the contractual-obligations total would move -- kill criterion 2 is built on that ratio rather than the gross commitment figure.

*One segment, one peer -- treated as a directional check, not a clean isolate.* Netflix reports as a single operating segment; the finest disclosed cut is region. Disney (DIS) is the closest scaled comparable with its own DTC streaming arm, but its consolidated 22.2x TTM P/E (an internally computed reading of Disney's own trailing GAAP earnings from build/universe.json, not itself a primary-filing figure) blends parks, linear television, studios and streaming -- it does not isolate what the market pays for a streaming platform alone, and no cleaner pure-play peer is covered by the engine's own universe (Spotify is not a name it tracks). Against that caveat, Netflix's normalized 28.4x still sits well above Disney's blended 22.2x, and Netflix's own margins and 30% ROIC argue for some premium -- but the comparison is one directional data point among several here, not a load-bearing case that 20-22x is the right Netflix multiple on its own.

*The Warner Bros. episode is a governance flag, not proof of discipline.* Netflix's board and management agreed to an $82.7B acquisition of WBD's studios and HBO Max/HBO, and the Feb. 27 8-K confirms it was structured to be financed substantially with debt: a bridge commitment letter (Dec. 4, 2025), an incremental-commitments agreement (Jan. 19, 2026), a senior unsecured revolving credit facility and a delayed-draw term loan (both Dec. 19, 2025) were all in place specifically to fund the merger consideration, and all four terminated automatically when the deal did. Collecting $2.8B rather than overpaying is a good outcome, but the willingness to arrange debt financing for a deal at roughly a quarter of Netflix's own market cap is itself evidence management saw a gap worth closing this way -- normalized FCF is not obviously headed entirely to buybacks, and a repeat attempt is a real capital-allocation risk (kill criterion 5), not a hypothetical one.

*The price.* At $79.59, fwd_at_hurdle_20x_exit is +9.4% (engine, on consensus non-GAAP forward EPS) against 26.9% trailing five-year CAGR delivered while the multiple collapsed -- favorable on its face, but section 3's table, built on the normalized $2.80 base, tells a less generous story once the fee is out and the two offsetting items are added back. Replay: 2 closed episodes, 100% beat SPY, median 12-month excess +31.6%, but on only 2 observations, and the one open episode (Oct. 2025 entry) is running only +6.3% so far -- thin, not dispositive either way.

The price is not offering a margin of safety even under a more generous, fully normalized earnings base, which is the case for wait over either a lean toward buying or an outright pass.

4. Kill criteria - pre-committed, falsifiable, dated

  1. Revenue growth guidance, tiered, with a corroboration test on the benign exception. Checked at the Q3 FY26 (~Oct. 20, 2026) and Q4 FY26 (~Jan. 2027) reports: Netflix's own guided next-quarter YoY revenue growth (as given, F/X-neutral where stated) below 11% -- review; below 9% -- re-underwrite. This uses Netflix's own guidance mechanic, which already compares each quarter to the same quarter a year earlier, so no period mismatch. Benign exception: a guided step-down the company attributes to a one-time content-slate or FX-timing shift is treated as benign only if operating margin guidance is unchanged AND growth recovers to at least the prior tier the following quarter; otherwise it counts toward the trip.
  2. Operating margin (clean) and a content-efficiency ratio in place of a fee-contaminated FCF subtraction. Checked at the Q3 FY26 report (~Oct. 20, 2026): FY2026 operating margin guidance (currently 31.5% vs 29.5% FY25 -- unaffected by the WBD fee, which sits below operating income) cut below 31.5% -- review; below 30% -- re-underwrite. The FCF branch no longer nets a fixed dollar figure against the $12.5B guide, since that guide cannot be cleanly reduced to an ex-fee number without an unquantified fee-related cash-tax adjustment (section 2). Instead: reported FY2026 FCF guidance cut below $11B AND the cash-content-spend-to-amortization ratio (currently ~1.1x guided) rising above 1.2x -- review; FCF guidance below $10B AND the ratio above 1.3x, or Netflix itself discloses a clean ex-fee FCF reconciliation showing a cut -- re-underwrite. Benign exception: a guided content-spend step-up tied to a named slate (e.g., live sport) with a stated payback window.
  3. Initial FY2027 guidance, with an explicit severe tier. Given at the Q4 FY26 report (typically ~Jan. 2027): moderate tier -- revenue growth midpoint below 10% OR operating margin guidance below 33% -- review; severe tier -- revenue growth below 8% OR operating margin below 31% -- re-underwrite directly; joint trip -- revenue below 10% AND margin below 33% together, even if neither alone reaches the severe tier -- re-underwrite. Benign exception: a deliberately conservative initial guide that still clears the moderate tier on both metrics is not the same as a guidance cut.
  4. View-hours annual disclosure, retained as corroborating evidence only, never a standalone trigger. Q1 2027, "What We Watched" annual edition: full-year 2026 total view-hours growth below +1.5% (2025: +1.5%; H1'26: +2%), measured against full-year 2025 actual. On its own this does not change the rating, since membership growth can lift aggregate hours while hours per member soften, and price-led pruning can lower hours while improving economics. It escalates a review to re-underwrite only if criterion 1 (revenue growth) or criterion 3 (initial FY2027 guidance) has independently tripped in the same year.
  5. Governance/capital-allocation tripwire, sized by risk, not only by deal value. An 8-K disclosing a new definitive merger or acquisition agreement, filed at any time before the next scheduled review, where EITHER enterprise value exceeds $10B, OR pro forma net debt/EBITDA would rise above 2.0x, OR financing includes equity issuance exceeding 5% of shares outstanding, OR the disclosure states no return-on-invested-capital rationale for a transaction above $2B -- re-underwrite immediately, regardless of price, given the WBD precedent in section 2. Benign exception: a tuck-in meeting none of those conditions (Netflix's own 2026 scale: the ~$587M and ~$1.87B-class deals already done) is ordinary business, not a repeat of the WBD attempt.
