## 1. What changed — why is the multiple where it is?

On Aug. 4, Par reported second-quarter net income of $462.1 million, or $9.35 per diluted share, versus $59.5 million, or $1.17, a year earlier. Adjusted net income was $499.2 million, or $10.10 per share. Refining adjusted gross margin rose $448.6 million to $680.4 million as the combined market index increased 139%. Higher crack spreads added $566.1 million, partly offset by $94.2 million of worse feedstock differentials. The filing does not attribute the increase to one geopolitical event; it identifies conflict with Iran and Strait of Hormuz disruptions only among forward-looking risks. The Hawaii turnaround was substantially complete by the release date. [Q2 release and 10-Q, filed Aug. 5, 2026]

On Aug. 25, Laramie Energy agreed to sell substantially all its oil and gas assets for $485 million cash. Par expects about $146 million of proceeds, including $27.5 million deferred for five years, plus up to $30 million of earn-outs. The expected year-end closing would end Par's 46% investment and reduce net debt, subject to the final gain or loss against its $43.3 million carrying value. [Laramie 8-K, filed Aug. 25, 2026]

## 2. Is the impairment real?

The franchise is not impaired, but the earnings base is. Q2 segment adjusted EBITDA was $552.0 million in refining, $29.8 million in logistics and $17.3 million in retail, less $27.9 million in corporate costs. Retail EBITDA fell from $23.3 million because lower fuel margins reduced gross margin by $3.1 million. Stable segments cannot offset a refining reversal.

Hawaii's margin rose to $57.00 per barrel from $10.18 as its index rose to $46.06 from $8.57, but $76.5 million, or $11.49 per barrel, was favorable price lag. Lag-adjusted margin was about $45.51. A separate $186.7 million FIFO benefit remained in refining results. Montana rose to $37.22 from $22.30 on higher throughput; Washington rose to $20.31 from $11.47 and Wyoming to $34.03 from $18.57. Mainland gains show broader crack widening alongside Hawaii's larger Singapore-linked move.

The adjusted-income bridge removes $105.2 million of favorable marks but adds back $11.5 million of debt costs and $122.3 million of deferred-tax items. First-half current tax was $23.4 million versus $133.0 million deferred. Adjusted income uses an NOL-shielded current-tax basis; a normal rate would reduce it. Three-year average adjusted income is $304.2 million, or $6.07 on 50.1 million shares, supporting rounded $6.00 EPS with optimistic tax treatment.

HF Sinclair is the closer structural peer. At the Sept. 1 secondary-market close of $106.06, it traded at 25.9x three-year average GAAP EPS of $4.09, versus PARR at 13.0x three-year average GAAP EPS of $6.17. Both use the same accounting basis, but DINO's multiple does not make PARR cheap on absolute returns.

TTM FCF is $412.4 million, or 10.3%, and $395.6 million, or 9.9%, after $16.8 million of SBC. Q2 working capital absorbed $312.2 million, depressing FCF and making the $35.28 P/FCF rung too low. On $523 million of mid-cycle EBITDA, less about $240 million of annual capex and turnaround spending and $60 million of interest, pre-tax FCF is roughly $220 million, or 5.5%, before normal tax. Share count fell 18.9% over three years.

The 2023-2025 10-K reconciliation averages to $523 million of EBITDA, but 2023 includes only seven months of Montana ownership and understates today's asset base; Laramie income will leave after closing and partly offsets that mismatch. A favorable $575 million sensitivity captures full-period Montana ownership. Small index moves produce large EBITDA swings, so either point estimate is imprecise. Management guidance and consensus growth could not be verified.
