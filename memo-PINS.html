## 2. Is the impairment real?

**Normalized earnings, and its assumption.** TTM reported diluted EPS is $0.34 versus FY2024's $2.67, a
comp effect above. Adding back the $61.4 million of H1 2026 restructuring charges (10-Q Note 11) gives
normalized EPS of about $0.45 -- my own estimate, not an engine field, with no tax shield assumed because
the 10-Q reports income taxes as "not material" in the loss quarters (Note 9). Of that $61.4 million, $14.1
million is restructuring SBC already counted in the TTM SBC used below -- a small overlap, not one that
moves $0.45 much.

**SBC-adjusted FCF, tax and working-capital checks finished.** Reported TTM FCF is $1,280.4 million (9.7%
yield); netting the full $1,021.8 million of TTM SBC (section 0) gives $258.6 million (2.0% yield, ~51x) --
the conservative reading, since $207.7 million of that SBC generated a realized cash tax benefit already
inside reported cash flow (10-K: $182.3 million FY2025 less $86.0 million in H1 2025; 10-Q: $111.4 million
in H1 2026). A tax-adjusted netting ($1,021.8M-$207.7M) gives $466.3 million, a 3.6% yield -- still well
under 9.7%. On working capital: H1 2026 got a $197.4 million lift from AR collections and accrued-liability
growth versus $121.0 million in H1 2025 (10-Q MD&A), but that reverses against a $239.7 million AR build in
H2 2025 (FY2025 10-K); across the actual TTM window the two items net to about -$12.6 million, roughly
neutral -- so the reported figure is not meaningfully working-capital-inflated, though the half-over-half
swing alone would suggest otherwise.

**Is the own-history read comparable?** The engine's 5.7th-percentile flag and its one closed 36-month
replay episode (started 2023-05-03, itself at the 13.6th percentile) are measured on P/S and P/FCF, which
do not net SBC. That is like-for-like only if SBC ran a similar share of revenue then and now; it did not:
FY2023-FY2025 ran 20.9%-21.2%, H1 2026 ran 25.4% (TTM 22.4%, since H2 2025 alone was below trend at 19.7%).
Today's multiple carries a larger non-cash addback than the 2023 entry did. This does not prove PINS is
cheap or expensive against a true SBC-adjusted history -- that series is not rebuilt here, which would be
recomputing engine arithmetic by hand -- so neither the percentile nor the replay's -86% median excess
should be read as literal evidence about today. What the filings do support in absolute terms: a ~51x
SBC-adjusted P/FCF and a ~52x normalized P/E (versus the ~11x non-GAAP forward P/E quoted around the
market, GuruFocus, Aug. 21, 2026, which excludes SBC entirely) are both full for a business guiding to
deceleration.

**Demand side.** MAUs +11% YoY, revenue +18% YoY, ARPU up in every geography (U.S./Canada +14%, Europe
+4%, Rest of World +21%; 10-Q). The flat-to-down blended "price of ads" (+1% Q2, -2% six months, against
+16%/+20% more impressions) is a Rest-of-World mix effect -- ARPU there is $0.23 versus $8.30 in the U.S.
-- not lost per-market pricing power. One reported segment (10-Q Note 1), so no decomposition applies.

**Convert versus buyback, separated.** The $1.0 billion Notes added net debt and funded the ASR; the $3.5
billion program is ordinary open-market buying from cash flow, not debt. Only $1.0 billion of the $2,024.9
million spent on H1 2026 repurchases was debt-funded. The 54.8 million ASR shares were about 8% of the
678.1 million shares outstanding at Sept. 30, 2025 (corrected from 15%); all three H1 programs together
retired about 16%. Per 10-Q Note 8, the Notes settle in cash up to principal and dilute only when the
average price exceeds $22.72.

**Peer, same date, same method.** PINS's EV/Revenue is 2.85x (EV $13.0 billion less $274.9 million net

market."

## 3. Verdict and entry

flag nor the 36-month replay's -86% median excess (a 2023 entry under a lower SBC regime) is literal
evidence either way. That is enough not to treat the candidate flag's cheapness as established.

4. Kill criteria -- pre-committed, falsifiable, dated

Each criterion is checked against the named filing on the date shown, not against a portfolio-system score. (1) Quarterly SBC/revenue at or above 22% for two consecutive quarters without an improving after-SBC operating margin -- re-underwrite; at or above 24% for two consecutive quarters on the same condition -- treat the SBC step-up as durable and downgrade further (versus 20.9%-21.2% FY2023-FY2025 and H1 2026's 25.4%), checked at the Q3 2026 10-Q, the FY2026 10-K, and if unresolved, the Q1 2027 10-Q. (2) Q3 2026 revenue growth prints below the guided 13% floor -- re-underwrite, reading it alongside U.S./Canada ARPU growth, since a miss with intact per-user monetization differs from one with ARPU deceleration too. (3) Restructuring Plan charges exceed the disclosed $69.6 million cap, or a new restructuring action is announced -- an overrun beyond a ~10% timing true-up, or any new program, forces a re-underwrite of section 2's EPS normalization. (4) U.S./Canada ARPU growth prints at or below 0% for two consecutive quarters -- downgrade, as the first direct sign of per-market pricing-power loss in the highest-value geography. (5) TTM SBC-adjusted FCF per diluted share fails to improve from the current ~$0.46 run rate across two consecutive quarterly prints despite revenue growing at or above the guided rate -- downgrade further, since that would mean growth is not converting into real per-share cash economics regardless of the SBC/revenue ratio's path.
