## 2. Is the impairment real?

**Not a single fraction. What is filed supports three separate claims, not one number: the
2020-2022 base was an unsustainable pandemic peak (structural normalization, largely complete);
new-construction and remodel demand is cyclically weak (real, but not shown to be permanent); and a
genuine loss of pricing power, customer relevance or supplier access is NOT demonstrated in any
filed data point checked this round. The Aug. 21 review's "too generous" critique and the Aug. 25
review's "false precision" critique are both right about the prior "about half" framing - this
revision drops the fraction and states the three claims separately instead.**

**Units and revenue.** New in-ground construction, management's estimates: 117,000 units in 2021,
98,000 in 2022, 62,000 in 2024, just under 60,000 in 2025 (FY2025 10-K, citing industry data).
Revenue was not down proportionally - down 14% from the 2022 peak of $6.18B, then within 1% of
$5.3B for three years. Versus 2019: revenue $5.36B TTM vs $3.20B, operating income $585M vs $341M,
operating margin 10.9% vs 10.7% - the 2022 peak (16.6% margins, $18.60 EPS) was the anomaly, not
today's base.

**But the 2019-to-now comparison is a level check, not an organic-growth read, and the filed
bridge says something more specific and more recent.** The FY2025 10-K's own MD&A ranks the
drivers of FY2025 net sales (flat versus FY2024) in order of estimated magnitude: (1) stable
maintenance product sales, (2) lower sales volume of new-construction and other discretionary
products, and (3) a roughly 2-3% benefit from inflationary product cost increases, partially
offset by about 1% price deflation on certain items. Q2 2026's own release describes its +2% sales
growth the same way - "benefits from inflation, steady maintenance activity and improved sales of
building materials." Read plainly: underlying unit volume has been flat to slightly negative for
at least two years, and inflation plus a stable-dollar maintenance base is doing real work in the
reported top line - a materially more specific finding than the 2019-versus-now level comparison
in the prior draft, and one this memo could not have made without reading the 10-K's own sales-
driver bullet list rather than assuming it was not disclosed.

**The mix argument holds, but the 64% is not one uniform block, and this memo cannot pin down
exactly how much of it is deferrable.** The FY2025 10-K's risk factors put maintenance and minor
repair at approximately 64% of net sales, remodel at about 22%, and new construction at about 14%.
Pool and hot tub chemicals - approximately 14% of total 2025 net sales (10-K product-category
disclosure) - sit at the least-deferrable end of that range: a pool needs chemicals to stay safe to
use regardless of discretionary spending. The rest of the maintenance category is equipment repair
and replacement (pumps, filters, heaters, cleaners); the 10-K discloses no repair-frequency,
replacement-age or failure-rate data that would say how much of that spend can actually be
deferred versus how much fails and forces a purchase. The defensible range is therefore between
"all 64% is recurring" and "only the 14% chemicals slice is," not a single narrower figure - the
prior draft's implicit claim that chemicals alone are non-deferrable substituted judgment for
disclosed evidence, per the Aug. 25 review. The 2021 10-K's own note that maintenance rose to
"~70%" of sales at the 2006-2010 trough is evidence the CATEGORY MIX shifts toward maintenance in a
downturn because discretionary spend falls faster, not evidence that maintenance dollars themselves
are immune to one. The installed base (about 5.5 million in-ground pools, 10-K) is real and still
growing, which is the actual argument for resilience - not the 64% figure read as if it were all
chemicals, and not a narrower figure this memo cannot support either.

**Gross margin: a bridge, not a binary.** FY2025 gross margin was 29.7%, flat to FY2024's reported
29.7% but +20 bp on a like-for-like basis once FY2024's one-time 20 bp benefit from a $12.6M
import-tax reversal is excluded (10-K MD&A) - management's own attribution is "positive impacts
from price increases and disciplined supply chain management," not share loss. Q2 2026 gross
margin fell 30 bp to 29.7% (six months: 29.4%, also -30 bp), which the company attributes
"primarily" to "elevated inbound freight costs and changes in customer mix," partially offset by
supply-chain initiatives - a cost-line and mix story, not a stated price concession, and FY2026
guidance still expects gross margin only about 30 bp below FY2025's. None of the three most recent
filed data points shows a pricing-power loss; the kill criteria below still treat a further move
as a trigger, because the filed attribution could turn out to be optimistic, not because it
already has.

**The distribution moat, underwritten directly, not assumed.** POOL is, by its own description,
"the largest wholesale distributor of swimming pool and related backyard products ... and one of
the only national wholesale distributors focused on the swimming pool industry" (FY2025 10-K, Item
1), operating 456 sales centers and about 125,000 customers with no single customer at or above
10% of 2025 sales - real scale and real diversification. But the same section states plainly that
"barriers to entry in our industry are relatively low," and names the competitive factors as
breadth of product, service quality, marketing-program availability, relationship consistency,
price and geographic proximity - scale and execution advantages, not patents, network effects or
switching costs. Supplier concentration cuts the other way: Pentair, Zodiac and Hayward supplied
about 20%, 12% and 11% of 2025 cost of products sold respectively (43% combined) - real
bargaining-power exposure to three vendors. POOL360 and Horizon 24/7 (digital B2B ordering) are
genuine differentiation for a fragmented base of small, family-owned customers who are "highly
reliant" on POOL's product breadth and support (10-K), but nothing in the filing describes them as
exclusive or hard to replicate. Net: the moat is real and is exactly what the company says it is -
scale, density and service in a low-barrier industry - worth a premium to a no-scale regional
distributor, not a premium that explains a 20x-plus multiple on its own; it is consistent with,
not contrary to, a ~30% gross margin that looks like a distributor's margin because POOL is a
distributor. POOL reports one segment; the finest disclosed decomposition is the product-category
and end-use mix used throughout this section, not a segment table.

**Growth: the algorithm management withdrew is the one input the multiple needs.** Trailing 5-year
EPS CAGR +0.4% (section 0). Watwood, July 23 call (secondary transcript - could not verify), on
the historical 6-9% algorithm: "under current market conditions, I don't think the six to nine is
on the table." ROIC 16.6% versus 28.8% three years ago (section 0). The empty `caveats` field on
the fair_band is generous: the ten-year window holds a 52.35x maximum from 2020-21, and a
no-growth distributor does not earn a 29.79x median multiple. The mid rung ($324.28) is not a
target; even the p20 rung ($251.03, 33.5% above spot) sits above where a buyer would actually pay
for the growth on offer.

**SBC-adjusted FCF, and net share count versus gross buyback yield.** Reported TTM FCF is $312.7M,
about 4.5% of the roughly $6.9B market cap implied by section 0's own yield math; SBC-adjusted FCF

4. Kill criteria

  1. FY2026 adjusted diluted EPS guidance midpoint (ex-CEO-transition costs), paired with the price/volume bridge, tiered. EPS alone is a lagging aggregate repurchases can support even as unit demand weakens (Aug. 25 review), so this criterion checks the guidance number AND whether management's own quarterly sales-driver commentary (section 2's price/volume bridge - the same "stable maintenance, lower discretionary volume, X% inflation" language POOL gives every release) still shows inflation, not volume, doing the work. Current guide $10.87-$11.17 (midpoint $11.02, raised July 23, 2026 from $10.85-$11.15). Midpoint holds at or above $10.87 (today's own low end) AND the sales-driver commentary is unchanged in kind: intact. Midpoint cut to $10.50-$10.87, OR the commentary shifts to volume/unit deterioration beyond what inflation explains: review. Midpoint below $10.50: downgrade/re-underwrite regardless of the commentary. Checked at the Oct. 22, 2026 Q3 print, confirmed at the FY2026 10-K (~Feb. 2027).
  2. Year-to-date (through Q3) gross margin, like-for-like against the same nine months of 2025, tiered against the company's OWN guided pace, not a flat line. FY2026 guidance itself implies full-year gross margin near 29.4% (about 30 bp below FY2025's 29.7%); H1 2026 was already there (29.4%). Nine-month 2026 at or above 29.4%: intact. 28.9%-29.4% (up to 50 bp worse than what management already guided): review. Below 28.9% (more than 50 bp worse than guided): downgrade. Independent trigger, checked the same date: gross profit DOLLARS (not just the percentage) decline year over year despite reported sales growth - review, even if the margin percentage alone would not have tripped, since a stable percentage can mask a real dollar-level erosion if mix shifts. Checked at the Oct. 22, 2026 Q3 print.
  3. Recurring maintenance-and-minor-repair revenue, two independent legs, full fiscal year against full fiscal year only (never an interim quarter, since POOL discloses this mix annually, not quarterly). FY2025 baseline: 64% of net sales ($5,289.4M total), an estimated ~$3.4B in the category (64% x total sales - this dollar figure is this memo's own arithmetic, not a line item POOL discloses). Leg A (independent, catches a broad demand contraction the mix percentage alone could hide): the derived absolute maintenance-category revenue is below the FY2025 level - review, regardless of what the percentage does. Leg B (confirms a mix/share shift specifically): the mix ALSO falls below 60% of net sales at the same time - escalates Leg A's review to a downgrade. A mix decline driven by a remodel or new-construction RECOVERY (rising absolute remodel/new-construction dollars, maintenance dollars flat or growing) trips neither leg. Checked once, at the FY2026 10-K (~Feb. 2027).
  4. Rescheduled Investor Day, or the FY2026 10-K (~Feb. 2027) if none is held by then - a re-underwriting checkpoint, not a standalone kill on its own. The event itself does not prove or disprove anything (a framework can be published without being achievable, and its absence can reflect governance caution rather than economic deterioration - Aug. 25 review); what actually moves the tier is criteria 1-3 above, re-evaluated at that point using whatever concrete organic sales, gross-profit and capital-allocation numbers the framework or the 10-K discloses. A framework implying contraction, or a second postponement with no new date: downgrade.
  5. Leverage and capital allocation, two legs, using the seasonality-resistant covenant ratio, not a point-in-time figure. Section 0's net_debt_to_ebitda (2.58x) is a point-in-time net-debt read that POOL's own seasonal inventory financing (built ahead of peak season, per section 2) could move quarter to quarter without any real change in credit risk, so it is NOT the trigger here - it is context only. Above 2.75x for two consecutive quarters, or any single quarter above 3.0x: downgrade/re-underwrite. Poor conversion still triggers Leg B's review even if management prudently pauses repurchases, since weak conversion is an earnings-quality question independent of capital allocation (Aug. 25 review). FY2025 conversion was about 0.90 (one year so far); H1 2026 repurchases already slowed to $86.4M from $160.6M a year earlier while debt still rose $110.8M - worth watching, not yet a trigger under either leg.
