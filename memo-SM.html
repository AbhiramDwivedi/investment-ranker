The memo argues with the quality failure and historical band: merger consideration caused the share-count break, while the old per-share history and five poor replay episodes describe legacy SM, not the combined asset base or balance sheet.

## 1. What changed - why is the multiple where it is?

On Jan. 30, 2026, SM closed its all-stock Civitas merger. The June 10-Q records 123,715,771 merger shares, changing the production base and leverage. [Jan. 30 8-K; Aug. 6 10-Q]

On April 30, SM sold 61,000 net South Texas acres for $896 million of net proceeds. Q2 included a $262 million divestiture gain and about $70 million of severance-tax refund income. [Aug. 5 release; Aug. 6 10-Q]

On Aug. 5, SM raised second-half production guidance to 435-440 MBOE/d, held 2026 capital guidance at $2.65-$2.85 billion and cut recurring G&A guidance by $50 million at the midpoint. Net debt was $6.253 billion after a $1.1 billion sequential reduction. Q2 pre-hedge oil realization was $96.85/Bbl, but settlements cut it to $80.62/Bbl and cost $220 million. [Aug. 5 release]

The low trailing multiple reflects commodity exposure, merger leverage and uncertainty that integration gains will become durable per-share free cash flow.

## 2. Is the impairment real?

The impairment is real enough to reject a mean-reversion thesis. Pro-forma results as if the merger closed Jan. 1, 2025 show H1 2026 production revenue of $3.937 billion versus $3.871 billion, but net income of $805 million versus $842 million and diluted EPS of $3.35 versus $3.51. The engine's 215.3% revenue growth is merger accounting, not organic growth. [Aug. 6 10-Q]

Commodity exposure is the first mechanism. Q2 realization was $53.86/Boe before hedges and $48.36 after them. June 30 contracts covered 6.398 million Q3 oil barrels with $63.30 swaps and 5.662 million with $59.51/$69.09 collars, about 57% of guided Q3 oil volume. For 2027, 10.530 million swap barrels at $68.09 and 12.133 million collar barrels at $63.42/$72.07 cover roughly one-quarter of annualized Q3 volume. SM was capped on much of 2026 upside but has limited 2027 downside protection. [Aug. 5 release; Aug. 6 10-Q]

Basin revenue exposes the operating mechanism: Q2 production revenue was Permian $1.110 billion, DJ $579 million, Uinta $286 million and South Texas $181 million. Permian gas revenue was negative $53 million because of Waha exposure. LOE of $6.71/Boe was within $6.50-$6.80 guidance, so the sequential rise alone is not impairment. The filed efficiency benchmark is about $710 per lateral foot. [Aug. 5 release; Aug. 6 10-Q]

Q2 adjusted FCF was $467 million and H1 was $487 million. Adding back $42 million of one-time cash costs but removing the $70 million refund at the company's 22.9% adjustment tax rate gives about $455 million, or about $1.8 billion annualized. That is a scenario starting point, not normalized mid-cycle output. TTM reported FCF was $2.700 billion versus $2.648 billion after SBC. Q2 repurchases exceeded SBC and reduced shares from 239.7 million to 237.5 million. [Aug. 5 release; Aug. 6 10-Q]

Current Sept. 9 secondary market data show Matador at 10.4x, Chord at 10.0x and Ovintiv at 18.4x trailing P/E. Those are named checks, not valuation anchors: SM's $6.253 billion net debt is 41% of its $15.29 billion enterprise value. SM is about 2.7x enterprise value to annualized peak-quarter adjusted EBITDAX, but comparable current peer EV/EBITDAX could not be verified. The historical replay is also weak, but its five episodes used legacy SM's roughly 114 million-share asset base; the merger makes them less comparable, not favorable evidence.
