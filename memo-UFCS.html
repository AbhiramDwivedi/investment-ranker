## 1. What changed — why is the multiple where it is?

On Aug. 4, 2026, UFCS reported Q2 net income of $33.4 million versus $22.9 million and diluted EPS of $1.29 versus $0.87. The combined ratio improved to 95.3% from 96.4%, while the underlying combined ratio worsened 0.1 percentage point to 92.6%. Catastrophe losses fell to 2.7 points from 5.5 points, but neutral consolidated reserve development netted adverse movements in four lines against improvement in other liability. Net investment income supplied $7.3 million, or 57%, of the $12.7 million increase in pretax income as the fixed-income yield rose to 4.57% from 4.32%. The shares are 0.1% below their 52-week high and 29.1% above their 200-day average. The 10.2x TTM P/E therefore reflects elevated cyclical earnings, not a fresh selloff, while the price is about 1.44x the engine's $37.74 book value.

## 2. Is the impairment real?

UFCS reports one operating segment, but its business lines reveal mixed economics. Core commercial's Q2 net loss ratio improved to 59.5% from 61.9%. Workers' compensation net earned premium grew 43.6% while its net loss ratio worsened 15.0 points to 71.1%. Renewal data imply workers' compensation pricing lagged the rest of core commercial. Rapid growth into a softening line is the clearest impairment risk. Assumed reinsurance generated 14.7% of Q2 earned premium, and its loss ratio worsened 4.1 points to 65.6%. The half-year reinsurance loss ratio worsened 5.9 points to 69.3% because of mix and adverse development. Commercial auto, workers' compensation, surety and assumed reinsurance all cited worse reserve development, despite the consolidated zero.

The flat underlying combined ratio was not caused by loss deterioration. The underlying loss ratio improved 0.4 point, while the expense ratio rose 0.5 point because of real-estate footprint reduction. The half-year underlying combined ratio improved 1.2 points, partly because the prior-year period included final policy-system development costs. These discrete expenses temper both quarterly and half-year comparisons. Investment income is more durable: Q2 fixed-income yield improved as the portfolio grew.

Reported TTM EPS is $5.38. Catastrophe loads were 6.2, 5.4 and 3.2 points in 2023 through 2025, each below the disclosed 10-year average, versus 2.2 points TTM. Restating the TTM load to the 4.9-point three-year average reduces EPS by about $1.13 after tax to normalized EPS of about $4.25. On normalized earnings and roughly $959 million of average equity, normalized ROE is about 11.5%. That supports about 1.0x to 1.2x the filing's $38.02 June book value, or $38 to $46, consistent with the engine's P/B band and below spot.

UFCS bought back no shares in 2023 through Q2 2026. The 2.6% three-year share increase therefore had no repurchase offset, although Q2 diluted weighted-average shares fell 1.9% year over year. The dividend rose 25% to $0.20 quarterly. As a peer check, Selective Insurance traded at 11.32x TTM earnings on Aug. 31, 2026, according to secondary data, but P/B against ROE is the more useful insurer check. UFCS's smaller scale and reserve history do not support its current premium to normalized book value. The engine's 4.2% implied growth is achievable, but it is calculated at the less appropriate 9% tier and on reported EPS. The replay has only two episodes, a 50% hit rate and 9.9% median excess return, which is too thin to overturn the normalized valuation.

## 3. Verdict and entry

**wait** - underwriting remains profitable, but normalized earnings, book value and deterioration in fast-growing workers' compensation and assumed reinsurance do not support spot.
