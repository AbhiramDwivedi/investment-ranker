The memo argues with the engine's quality_compounder 9% hurdle and with using reported TTM EPS of $5.38 as the earnings base, because 2023 produced a 109.3% combined ratio and current TTM earnings are catastrophe- and prior-year-development-flattered.

## 1. What changed — why is the multiple where it is?

On Aug. 4, 2026, UFCS filed Q2 net income of $33.4 million versus $22.9 million and diluted EPS of $1.29 versus $0.87. The combined ratio improved to 95.3% from 96.4%, but the underlying combined ratio worsened 0.1 point to 92.6%. Catastrophe losses fell to 2.7 points from 5.5 points. Net investment income supplied $7.3 million, or 57%, of the $12.7 million pretax-income increase as the fixed-income yield rose to 4.57% from 4.32%. Workers' compensation earned premium grew 43.6% while its net loss ratio worsened 15.0 points, although the filing attributes that deterioration to prior-year development and says the underlying loss ratio improved. H1 ceded earned premium fell 23.1%, so part of the 11.9% net earned premium growth came from retaining more risk. An Aug. 21 8-K maintained the $0.20 quarterly dividend and added a director; it did not change the operating thesis.

## 2. Is the impairment real?

The headline 10.2x multiple is flattered. Reported TTM EPS is $5.38 and the TTM catastrophe load is about 2.2 points. Restating catastrophes to the 4.9-point 2023-2025 average reduces EPS to about $4.25. That remains favorable because UFCS says each annual catastrophe load in 2023 through 2025, including 6.2 points in 2023, was below its 10-year average. Applying the same three-year normalization to prior-year non-catastrophe development reduces the base again to about $3.67. A reasonable range is therefore $3.29 to $4.25, making spot about 12.9x to 16.7x normalized earnings rather than 10.2x.

The impairment risk is reserve adequacy and retained exposure, not proven deterioration in current workers' compensation accident-year economics. Workers' compensation pricing lagged the rest of core commercial while premium grew rapidly, but the filing says its underlying loss ratio improved. H1 ceded written and earned premiums fell 17.6% and 23.1%, respectively, so UFCS retained more underwriting risk while the most volatile earnings inputs remained unusually favorable.

Commercial other liability is the larger warning. It produced 32.6% of Q2 earned premium and a 65.7% net loss ratio, but it also caused $52.9 million of 2023 adverse development in excess casualty, umbrella and construction defect. Assumed reinsurance contributed 14.7% of Q2 earned premium and its loss ratio worsened to 65.6%. Those lines make the reserve tail structural enough to require evidence, even though current reported results improved. Two own-history cheap episodes beat SPY only half the time, with a median 12-month excess of -1.0%; the Q2 filing does not show a different reserve or cession regime now.

Book value is the cleaner cross-check. UFCS is at 1.44x June book value of $38.02. Peer Selective Insurance (SIGI) is at about 1.52x its filed $58.13 book value using the Sept. 16 factor-row close of $88.53, but Selective reported 13.7% non-GAAP operating ROE to 14.8% ROE. UFCS does not deserve a similar P/B without similar through-cycle returns. The blended own-history band of $29.44 to $46.80 is the P/B leg; the P/E-leg low of $52.67 uses the flattered $5.38 TTM and is not a floor. UFCS made no repurchases in 2023 through H1 2026. H1 stock compensation rose 43% to $5.3 million, and the three-year share count rose 2.6%, so issuance was not offset by buybacks.

## 3. Verdict and entry

**wait** - cheapness is earned on a flattered TTM, spot sits above the machine band and normalized earnings do not clear a 12% hurdle.

Entry: the machine band is $29.44 / $37.36 / $46.80 on pe and pb. Spot $54.82 sits above the $46.80 high, so there is no armed-style entry at the current price. The $52.67 P/E-leg low rests on reported $5.38 TTM rather than the $3.67 through-cycle base. Reassess after Q3 2026 results disclose reserve development, line-level loss ratios and cessions.
