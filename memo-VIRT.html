The memo argues with the apparent 9.5x trailing P/E, not with the engine's conclusion: VIRT sits at the 74.1st percentile of its blended own-history valuation, above the $47.97 mid-band rung, while the weak replay warns that a low P/E alone has not reliably produced excess returns.

## 1. What changed - why is the multiple where it is?

On July 30, Virtu reported second-quarter revenue of $1.190 billion, up 19.0%, and adjusted net trading income, or ANTI, of $717.9 million, up 26.4%. Market Making ANTI rose 28.4% to $579.9 million, while Execution Services ANTI rose 18.7% to $138.0 million. GAAP EPS slipped to $1.63 from $1.65 because the prior-year quarter included the RFQ-hub sale gain and current compensation accruals rose with profitability. First-half trading income rose 32.4%, leaving 72.0% of first-half revenue tied to trading income and exposing the cycle. The July 31 10-Q also records that the SEC withdrew proposed Rule 615 and Regulation Best Execution in June 2025. The live June 11, 2026 proposal would instead rescind Rules 611 and 610(e), which management says could hurt or help the business. Amended Rule 605 execution-quality reporting took effect Aug. 1, 2026 and creates the nearer-term test of competitive capture.

## 2. Is the impairment real?

The franchise is not presently impaired, but $6.02 of trailing GAAP EPS is a peak-cycle result rather than a through-cycle base. Virtu earned $2.97 in 2024 and $5.13 in 2025 as annual ANTI rose from $1.598 billion to $2.145 billion; averaging those EPS figures with the current $6.02 gives $4.71. I use $4.75 as normalized EPS because it also represents ANTI moving from the elevated trailing run rate toward roughly $8.5 million per day. JNX is not the normalization issue: the 10-Q quantifies a $5.3 million Q2 gain and a $0.6 million first-half loss, both immaterial beside the cycle.

Market Making supplied 81% of Q2 ANTI. The first warning is consolidated daily ANTI and, more importantly, Rule 605 price improvement and effective-spread performance relative to other wholesalers at similar market volumes. Execution Services is the offset: its ANTI rose 18.7% in Q2 and 24.2% in the first half, while its Q2 commissions and technology revenue rose 17.3%. A trailing-year decline there would indicate structural weakness rather than normal volatility.

The valuation bases disagree. The 74.1st percentile and the $37.10/$47.97/$59.06 band come from P/B, while P/E is at the 39.7th percentile with a $44.85/$66.40/$120.76 band. Peak returns inflate P/B while peak earnings depress P/E. P/S and P/FCF put spot above their high rungs, but P/FCF is unreliable for a broker-dealer: first-half operating cash flow was $117.0 million and cash after capitalized software and equipment spending was about $60.5 million despite $631.5 million of net income. The engine's reported TTM FCF of $521.1 million and SBC-adjusted $397.3 million are window artifacts, not valuation inputs. First-half SBC rose 52.5% to $67.8 million, though diluted shares fell 7.8% over three years.

StoneX is the closer peer because its execution and principal-risk earnings are also volume-sensitive. The same Sept. 17 engine run shows SNEX at 15.8x trailing EPS versus VIRT at 9.5x. VIRT deserves a discount for the peak earnings base, but that gap confirms the pass rests on normalization, not on VIRT being optically expensive. Company-normalized Q2 EPS of $1.82 uses about 159.9 million fully exchanged units, unlike GAAP EPS on about 87.6 million diluted Class A shares, so the measures are not directly stackable.

## 3. Verdict and entry

**pass** - normalized earnings show that spot already prices a favorable market-making regime, while the base case returns only 1.9% a year including the dividend.

Entry: none. The $37.10 low rung is an extreme-distress reference, not a live limit; conflicting P/E and P/B bands make it unsuitable. Reconsider after two amended Rule 605 reports show stable relative execution quality and a quarterly filing supports normalized EPS above $4.75. The quality_compounder tier requires a 9% return, which the base case misses.
