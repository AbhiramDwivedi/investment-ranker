The memos

The table ranks. A memo answers the one question arithmetic cannot: is the cheapness explained? Each one takes a single company, says what changed, argues whether the damage is cyclical or structural, and ends with a verdict and the criteria that would kill it.

A memo is dated and it stays dated. It is the view on the day it was written, at that day's price and on that day's filings, and nothing re-checks it afterwards. Read the date first. Anything older than 90 days carries a notice saying so.

What this is not. The ledger behind this site holds 309 decisions, collapsed from 2,306 name-nights, and none of them has ever been actionable. One row is one decision, not one appearance. A company suggested on 40 consecutive nights is counted once, dated from the first of them, so a name that keeps qualifying cannot inflate the sample. The cohorts above are the other view of the same calls and do repeat a name every night, by design. The actionable date is the first night every condition of the actionable-suggestion contract held at once. It reads empty for a name that has never been actionable, and for every night frozen before the contract existed on Aug. 23, 2026. The engine's current rules are forward-only from Aug. 16, 2026, so the record is days long, not years. This is a personal research tool, not licensed financial advice, and nothing here is a recommendation to buy or sell any security.
