What the score is: a 0-115 measure of how strongly the stock currently resembles patterns that preceded big gains in the past — built from its sales-growth rank and price-strength rank within all 1,500 stocks, plus the bonuses and penalties listed in each row's pop-up. What it is not: a buy rating — and that is by design, not a flaw. This table works in three layers: the pattern score decides where to look (pure arithmetic, the same formula for all 1,500 stocks, so it can be tested against history and can't quietly play favorites); the tags say what we found when we looked (judgments like "this trade is crowded" that can't be computed from a data feed — shown beside the score, never secretly subtracted from it); and a full investigation decides whether to buy (competition, the reason a stock is cheap, whether the filings' story holds — work no formula can do; tonight's cut, on the front page, already does the arithmetic on cash flow, debt and valuation against the company's own history). Everything measurable is in the math; everything that requires judgment is visible next to it; everything that requires investigation happens before any dollar moves.

This pattern flags stocks mid-run: by design, everything carrying it has already moved. Its record (2016-2025 replay, top-20 flags): 3.2% doubled within a year vs. 1.9% for the average index member; median 12-month return +12.6% vs. +10.4%. A real but modest edge — with a documented catastrophic mode: every top-20 flag from November 2021, the market's most euphoric month, was negative a year later (median about −45%).

Deep low, turning up — current rules (v2): at least 30% below its five-year high, its 52-week low set within the past six months, bounced 15-100% off that low, and a positive three-month return. The original version of this pattern FAILED its replay test: its top-20 flags doubled 5.8% of the time but fell 30%+ a full 21% of the time, with a median outcome of −4.1% while the average index member returned +10.4% — and it missed the decade's best recovery (Nvidia after October 2022) because its safety gate opened late. The v2 rules anchor to a recent low instead; they carry NO historical claim and are being judged on live data from Aug. 11, 2026. Until that verdict, treat these rows with the old warning.

New spin-off — split off from a parent within the past two years, judged on price strength (and sales growth once filings exist — young spin-offs have no year-ago quarter to compare for about a year, so v2 no longer requires it), plus a fixed 10-point bonus for the documented spin-off effect. Its record is the strongest and the least proven: 16.2% of top-20 flags doubled within a year, none halved, median +40% — but that comes from only 11 companies in nine years, dominated by three power-sector spins. Promising, tiny sample.

Scoring changelog — v2 went live Aug. 11, 2026, after the replay: trough-turn redesigned as above; growth flags now lose 10 points if the stock already doubled in the trailing year and 10 more after three straight quarters of accelerating sales (every November 2021 disaster carried both marks); spin-offs qualify before their filings exist; and the universe widened from the S&P 500 to the S&P 1500 — the replay showed the index boundary, not the scoring, is what hid Sandisk in 2025. The records quoted above were measured on v1; v2's record accrues live and will be reported as it comes.

Why Sandisk is No. 1 — and why that is not a buy signal

Ten of Sandisk's points are the fixed spin-off bonus. Even without it, it still leads a tight cluster (104.8 versus Sezzle's 104.1, Corcept's 103.8 and Acadian's 103.4) — its sales growth and six-month strength are at the ceiling of the whole 1,500-stock universe. But note who sits outside the top 20 because of v2's late-cycle penalties: AMD (94.8) and HPE (94.8), both docked 10 points for having already doubled in the trailing year — and, as of today, Datadog (94.0) for the same reason. Sandisk keeps its spot only because spin-offs are exempt from that penalty — a design choice the live forward test will judge.

Two more cautions. Sandisk has already gained about 162% in six months: the pattern was flagged mid-run, not early. And our own research (see "Scanned and passed" in the theme sleeves on the front page) calls memory chips the most crowded trade in the market right now. A stock can match a winning pattern and still be a poor buy at today's price. This page shows both facts rather than hiding either.

The replay added a finding: no version of this page could have flagged Sandisk in August 2025, because Sandisk did not join the S&P 500 until late 2025 — and its filings had no year-ago quarter to compute growth from until mid-2026. The index boundary, not the scoring, was the constraint. Expanding coverage beyond the S&P 500 is the roadmap answer.

What this list cannot see

Every pattern here requires movement that has already begun — sales accelerating, a stock climbing off a low. The list is blind to a coiled spring: a company whose order book is surging while its stock goes nowhere. Huntington Ingalls, Northrop Grumman, Constellation Energy and EMCOR — the names our industry research currently likes most — are exactly that shape, which is why they appear in the theme sleeves on the front page and not in this table.

If they start moving, the screen will catch them. The sleeves are how we try to be early instead of waiting for that.

Method. Deep-low scores: 0.6 × how far below the five-year high (percentile) + 0.25 × rebound size (percentile) + 15 if filings confirm the turn (sales accelerating or margins improving); the stock must be above a rising 200-day average, so falling knives are excluded by construction. Growth-inflection scores: 0.5 × growth percentile + 0.45 × six-month-return percentile + 10 if sales are accelerating. Spin-offs add a fixed 10. Fundamentals come from SEC filings, prices are daily closes and news tone is the average sentiment of the last roughly 50 articles per name (Alpha Vantage), scored on the top rows only.

Honesty box. No expected returns appear here because none can be computed honestly. The pattern records above come from a week-by-week replay of 2016-2026 using only information knowable on each date — index membership as of the date, filings by their filing date, prices through that Friday. Four caveats travel with those numbers. The model that designed these patterns was trained on this same history, so the replay can kill bad patterns but cannot prove good ones; a live forward test started Aug. 11, 2026, with immutable weekly snapshots. About 18% of companies that left the index have no retrievable price history, so the numbers are flattered by the survivors. The counts are stock-weeks, not independent bets — one stock flagged for 40 straight weeks is one episode. And in the six months after the model's knowledge ends, every pattern has so far trailed the average index member. Known data issues, flagged in the rows themselves: Sandisk's +251% and SM Energy's +215% sales-growth figures look like acquisition or spin-off accounting rather than organic growth and are under review; Chord Energy's +84% was checked and is real, but roughly 90% of it is a jump in realized crude prices, not more barrels. Waters is still in the table, at 94.9, and its +91% sales growth is confirmed merger accounting — the BD Biosciences deal, not underlying demand — so the row carries a Data check tag. Micron's +346% is still under review but matches no pattern today, so it does not appear.
